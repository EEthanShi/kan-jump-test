You are completing the design of a small book sorting row of carts with 3 carts.

The carts are: NIGI, ROBA, VOKA. Books move NIGI -> ROBA -> VOKA.
There are 3 moving steps:
- "giga" carries each NIGI tray to a ROBA tray.
- "rasi" is an internal rearrangement of ROBA: it carries each ROBA tray to a ROBA tray.
- "maza" carries each ROBA tray to a VOKA tray.

The row's operating rules. These must hold exactly, tray by tray:
R1.1 Running "giga" and then "rasi" lands on the same tray as running "giga" alone.
     ("rasi" leaves every tray that "giga" produces unchanged.)
R1.2 Running "rasi" and then "maza" gives the same result as running "maza" alone.
     ("maza" cannot tell whether "rasi" was applied first.)
R1.3 Running "rasi" twice in a row leaves every ROBA tray exactly where it started.

Recorded observations (fixed; your design must reproduce them exactly):
- NIGI has exactly 2 trays, called n1, n2.
- VOKA has exactly 1 tray, called s1.
- The observed end-to-end behavior of the row sends n1 to s1, n2 to s1.

Requirements for the middle cart (ROBA):
D1.1 ROBA must be a finite set with at most 4 trays.
D1.2 "rasi" must NOT be the do-nothing move (it must move at least one ROBA tray).
D1.3 "giga" must carry different NIGI trays to different ROBA trays.
D1.4 Every VOKA tray must be reached by "maza" from at least one ROBA tray.

Your task: give one complete concrete design of the middle cart.

Answer format bound: designs with up to 4 trays in the middle cart
are well-formed answers (the requirements above may restrict this further).

Answer in exactly this format (listing every tray and every table entry;
use tray names q1_1, q1_2, ... for the middle cart):

ROBA = {q1_1, ..., q1_k}
giga: n1 -> <ROBA tray>, n2 -> <ROBA tray>
rasi: q1_1 -> <ROBA tray>, ..., q1_k -> <ROBA tray>
maza: q1_1 -> <VOKA tray>, ... (one entry per ROBA tray)

Every rule and requirement above must hold exactly for your tables. Do not
explain; output only the lines in the format above.
