You are completing the design of a small book sorting row of carts with 6 carts.

The carts are: VUTA, KOZO, PIRA, DAGA, FOMU, RODA. Books move VUTA -> KOZO -> PIRA -> DAGA -> FOMU -> RODA.
There are 9 moving steps:
- "pifa" carries each VUTA tray to a KOZO tray.
- "zaba" is an internal rearrangement of KOZO: it carries each KOZO tray to a KOZO tray.
- "pika" carries each KOZO tray to a PIRA tray.
- "nubo" is an internal rearrangement of PIRA: it carries each PIRA tray to a PIRA tray.
- "suku" carries each PIRA tray to a DAGA tray.
- "pago" is an internal rearrangement of DAGA: it carries each DAGA tray to a DAGA tray.
- "liro" carries each DAGA tray to a FOMU tray.
- "raga" is an internal rearrangement of FOMU: it carries each FOMU tray to a FOMU tray.
- "vona" carries each FOMU tray to a RODA tray.

The row's operating rules. These must hold exactly, tray by tray:
R1.1 Running "pifa" and then "zaba" lands on the same tray as running "pifa" alone.
     ("zaba" leaves every tray that "pifa" produces unchanged.)
R1.2 Running "zaba" and then "pika" gives the same result as running "pika" alone.
     ("pika" cannot tell whether "zaba" was applied first.)
R1.3 Running "zaba" twice in a row leaves every KOZO tray exactly where it started.
R2.1 Running "pika" and then "nubo" lands on the same tray as running "pika" alone.
     ("nubo" leaves every tray that "pika" produces unchanged.)
R2.2 Running "nubo" and then "suku" gives the same result as running "suku" alone.
     ("suku" cannot tell whether "nubo" was applied first.)
R2.3 Running "nubo" three times in a row leaves every PIRA tray exactly where it started.
R3.1 Running "suku" and then "pago" lands on the same tray as running "suku" alone.
     ("pago" leaves every tray that "suku" produces unchanged.)
R3.2 Running "pago" and then "liro" gives the same result as running "liro" alone.
     ("liro" cannot tell whether "pago" was applied first.)
R3.3 Running "pago" twice in a row leaves every DAGA tray exactly where it started.
R4.1 Running "liro" and then "raga" lands on the same tray as running "liro" alone.
     ("raga" leaves every tray that "liro" produces unchanged.)
R4.2 Running "raga" and then "vona" gives the same result as running "vona" alone.
     ("vona" cannot tell whether "raga" was applied first.)
R4.3 Running "raga" three times in a row leaves every FOMU tray exactly where it started.

Recorded observations (fixed; your design must reproduce them exactly):
- VUTA has exactly one tray, called n1.
- RODA has exactly one tray, called s1.
- The observed end-to-end behavior of the row sends n1 to s1.

Requirements for the middle carts (KOZO, PIRA, DAGA, FOMU):
D1.1 KOZO must be a finite set with at most 3 trays.
D1.2 "zaba" must NOT be the do-nothing move (it must move at least one KOZO tray).
D2.1 PIRA must be a finite set with at most 4 trays.
D2.2 "nubo" must NOT be the do-nothing move (it must move at least one PIRA tray).
D3.1 DAGA must be a finite set with at most 3 trays.
D3.2 "pago" must NOT be the do-nothing move (it must move at least one DAGA tray).
D4.1 FOMU must be a finite set with at most 4 trays.
D4.2 "raga" must NOT be the do-nothing move (it must move at least one FOMU tray).

Your task: give one complete concrete design of the middle carts.

Answer format bound: designs with up to 5 trays per middle cart
are well-formed answers (the requirements above may restrict this further).

Answer in exactly this format (listing every tray and every table entry;
